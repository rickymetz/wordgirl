**Every word, a reflection.** Backwords gives you a bank of letters and a
mirror. Place letters on the left; the mirror spells the rest. Empty the whole
bank into valid rows and the day is done.

## How to play

There are two kinds of row:

- **Pair** — a word whose reversal is *also* a word (like STOP / POTS). Place
  one of them; its reflection appears on the other side. A pair consumes the
  full word's letters, and either orientation is a valid placement.
- **Palindrome** — a word that reads the same both ways (like LEVEL). You
  place only the first half, $\lceil n/2 \rceil$ letters; the mirror
  completes it. Odd-length palindromes put their middle letter *on* the
  mirror line — a straddle row.

Every day's bank decomposes in **more than one way**, and the ways differ in
row count — so there's a real choice between a greedy decomposition and an
efficient one.

The ✦ flourish marks a **glyph-true** row: one built only from letters whose
uppercase shapes survive a real vertical mirror — A H I M O T U V W X Y — so
a physical mirror would render exactly the reflection you see.

## Under the hood

### The lexicon

`src/games/backwords/engine/lexicon.ts` scans the common dictionary tier
once and builds a map from *placement string* to row definition:

- **Pairs**: every word whose reversal is a different valid word, entered
  under both orientations.
- **Palindromes**: entered by their placed half, shortest first, with
  **prefix aliases** so typing past one palindrome's half can reach a longer
  one (typing past POP's half "po" reaches POO and POOP).

Each row carries a `cost` — the sorted multiset of letters it consumes. The
generator and the play-time validator share this lexicon, so any accepted
puzzle is guaranteed solvable under exactly the player's rules.

### The solver: exact multiset partition

Emptying the bank is an **unordered exact-cover problem over letter
multisets**: pick a set of rows whose combined letter costs equal the bank
exactly. That problem is NP-hard in general, but banks are small enough that
exhaustive enumeration is instant.

`solveBank` does a DFS over the lexicon in index order — recursing only
forward from the current index, which makes solutions unordered and
repetition-free by construction — subtracting each row's cost from the bank's
letter counts and succeeding when the bank empties. With banks of at most 12
letters and rows costing at least 2, recursion depth is bounded around 6.
Enumeration caps at 40 solutions.

### Generation

Up to 400 attempts (`src/games/backwords/engine/generator.ts`):

1. Seed with one pair costing **4+ letters**, so every day has a meaty row.
2. Pad with random distinct lexicon items until the bank holds **8–12
   letters**. (The band was tightened from 12–16 in v2: it cut staple
   repetition sharply — the most common item appeared in 37% of days'
   solution spaces instead of 52% — while keeping generation robust.)
3. Solve the bank completely. Accept only if there are **at least 2 full
   decompositions with at least 2 distinct row counts**; prefer days with 3+
   solutions, keeping the first 2-solution candidate as a fallback.

The shipped puzzle is just the bank plus metadata (`solutionCount`,
`rowCounts`) — play-time validation is a lexicon lookup and multiset
arithmetic.
