**Every way the words fit.** Crosshatch shows a small crossword frame with a
few letters already placed. Unlike a normal crossword, there isn't one answer:
many different word combinations fit the frame, and your goal is to discover
**every word** that appears in *any* valid filling.

## How to play

- Fill the frame so that every line spells a dictionary word consistent with
  the given letters, then submit. A valid grid **banks every new word in
  it** — the unit of progress is the distinct word, not the full grid, so you
  never re-submit near-identical grids just to sweep a cross-product.
- Words can't repeat within one grid (crossword convention).
- The day counts as **solved at 90%** of the distinct words — with always at
  least two words of slack. Finding 100% is the flex above it, so one elusive
  word never breaks a streak.

The exact target (`src/games/crosshatch/engine/scoring.ts`):

$$
\text{target}(n) = \max\!\Big(1,\; \min\big(\lceil 0.9\,n \rceil,\; n-2\big)\Big)
$$

The $n-2$ clamp exists because a plain ceiling made "90%" mean "all but one"
on every 10–19-word day.

## Under the hood

Crossword filling is NP-hard in general. Crosshatch keeps it fast with tiny
hand-designed frames and classic CSP machinery
(`src/games/crosshatch/engine/generator.ts`).

### The shape library

Eight hand-designed skeletons — *h, ring, comb, rake, stairs, cross, lattice,
zipper* — each with 3–5 interlocking slots of 3–5 letters inside a 5×5 grid
(kept small for tappability on phones). The array's order and contents are
frozen: daily seeds index into it, so any edit would silently reshuffle
history.

### Generation: seed a solution, then tighten

1. **Random fill.** Find one complete valid filling by backtracking with the
   **most-constrained-variable (MRV)** heuristic — always fill the slot with
   the fewest candidates next — sampling only 40 shuffled candidates per slot
   to keep runtime flat. Candidate lookup uses a memoized positional index,
   `(length, position, letter) → words`: intersect constraints by scanning the
   *smallest* matching list and filter-checking the rest.
2. **Minimal givens.** Place one given letter per slot (a shared cell can
   cover two slots), guarded so no given ever completes a line outright.
3. **Enumerate everything.** An exhaustive DFS (`enumerateCombos`) lists
   *every* filling consistent with the givens, MRV-ordered, no word reused
   across slots, capped at `ENUM_CAP = 200` — past that the grid is too loose
   to be a puzzle.
4. **Tighten.** While the enumeration hits the cap, the distinct-word count
   exceeds 22, or any single slot admits more than 8 words, add another given
   — preferentially inside the "fattest" slot — up to 6 extras. Accept when
   the day lands at **10–22 distinct words** with at most one fully-locked
   slot. Over-tightened attempts are discarded; the generator retries up to
   300 times.

### Why play-time validation is free

The full combo list ships inside the puzzle object (it's regenerated from the
seed, never persisted). Checking a submitted grid is just a lookup against
the enumerated solution space — the expensive search all happened once, at
generation time.
