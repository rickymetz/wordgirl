**Spell your way from triangle to decagon.** Polygram starts you with three
letters arranged as a triangle. Find every 3-letter word those letters can
spell (letters may be reused) and a fourth letter joins — the triangle becomes
a square. Clear the square and it becomes a pentagon, and so on. A great day
runs all the way to the 10-sided decagon.

## How to play

- The polygon's vertices are your letters. At a size-*n* level, every answer
  is exactly *n* letters long and uses only those letters — repeats allowed.
- Clear **all required words** at a level to advance. Bonus words (rarer
  dictionary entries) count toward your score but aren't needed to advance.
- Stuck? Reveal a hint letter — but each revealed letter **halves that word's
  points** (it always stays worth at least 1).
- Scoring: an un-hinted word is worth its length; clearing a level adds a
  bonus equal to the level size. The maximum score assumes every required
  *and* bonus word found with no hints — so 100% measures exhaustive
  sweeping, not just advancement.

## Under the hood

The daily puzzle is built by **greedy incremental growth with rejection
sampling** (`src/games/polygram/engine/generator.ts`).

1. **Seed.** Pick a random 3-letter word from the dictionary and take its
   distinct letters. If it has repeats, pad from a frequency-weighted pool
   (`"eeeeaaaarrriii…"`) so common letters fill the gaps.
2. **Check the triangle.** Enumerate every 3-letter word spellable from the
   seed letters. The level must have 1–8 words, and every seed letter must
   appear in at least one of them.
3. **Grow.** For each size from 4 up to 10, shuffle the unused letters and
   try them one at a time. A candidate letter is accepted if the extended
   letter set yields a word count inside the level's band *and* the new
   letter actually appears in at least one word at that level — every join
   has to matter.
4. **Accept or retry.** A finished attempt is kept only if it reaches at
   least the pentagon (`MIN_MAX_LEVEL = 5`) and has at most 35 required
   words total. Otherwise the generator throws the attempt away and starts
   over — up to 300 times.

The per-level word caps taper as the polygon grows, because "find ALL the
words" is the advancement gate and long levels would become a slog:

| Level size | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 |
|-----------|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|----|
| Max words | 8 | 10 | 10 | 8 | 6 | 5 | 4 | 3 |

From the heptagon (7) up, levels also need at least **two** words — a
single-word nonagon ("find exactly ECOSYSTEM from nine blanks") gives no
partial traction.

### Why enumeration is fast

Every dictionary word carries a precomputed 26-bit **letter bitmask**. Testing
whether a word is spellable from the puzzle's letter pool is a single AND:

$$
\text{spellable} \iff (\textit{wordMask} \;\&\; \lnot\,\textit{poolMask}) = 0
$$

so enumerating a level is one linear scan over the length bucket with a
constant-time test per word (`src/lib/words/dictionary.ts`).

### Hint scoring

Revealed letters halve a word's value repeatedly, flooring at 1:

$$
\text{points}(w, h) = \max\!\left(1,\; \left\lfloor \frac{|w|}{2^{h}} \right\rfloor\right)
$$

where $|w|$ is the word's length and $h$ the number of hint letters revealed.
